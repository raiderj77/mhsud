# Privacy and Screening Readiness Review: Internal Operations Runbook

**Status:** Internal operating draft

**Verified against public offer:** 2026-08-29

**Purpose:** Deliver one bounded $495 founding-client review consistently without collecting health data or implying legal, clinical, security, accessibility, or licensing certification.

This runbook is not customer terms, a contract, legal advice, clinical guidance, a security standard, or an insurance determination. Owner-approved payment, cancellation, refund, tax, contract, insurance, and secure-delivery decisions remain separate release gates.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Use only public pages or a fictional, non-sensitive staging environment.
- Never request or accept patient records, assessment answers, scores, results, diagnoses, treatment decisions, crisis status, or other consumer health information.
- Never ask for production credentials, database access, analytics user-level records, session recordings, or customer-support transcripts.
- Inspect assessment entry states only. Do not answer questions, submit an assessment, or open a result screen.
- Do not reproduce restricted questionnaire items in notes or reports.
- Separate verified evidence, supported inference, and unknowns.
- Only an instrument owner or authorized publisher can grant instrument rights.
- Escalate legal, clinical, privacy, security, accessibility, and regulatory conclusions to professionals qualified for the exact scope.
- Do not promise compliance, certification, approval, safety, traffic, revenue, or a release outcome.
- Do not change or deploy the customer's product as part of this fixed scope.

## Stage 1: Qualify the inquiry

### Information allowed

- Organization name and public website.
- Contact name, business role, and business email.
- Public URLs or a fictional staging URL.
- Approximate number of routes.
- Instrument names and versions, if known, without questionnaire items or responses.
- The business question, planned release stage, and desired timeline.

### Fit checklist

The founding scope is a potential fit only when all answers are yes:

- [ ] The buyer is responsible for a digital-health or behavioral-health software journey.
- [ ] The review can use public pages or fictional, non-sensitive staging states.
- [ ] The scope is no more than five routes and one named screening journey or instrument family.
- [ ] The requested output is a gap register and prioritized readiness report.
- [ ] The buyer accepts that the review is not legal advice, clinical validation, instrument permission, penetration testing, formal WCAG conformance, or regulatory certification.
- [ ] No real health information, answers, scores, results, or patient records are needed.
- [ ] The requested timing is compatible with the stated five-business-day delivery window after scope and access are ready.

### Not a fit

Stop before quoting or accepting payment when the buyer requires:

- A legal opinion, HIPAA determination, regulatory certification, or contract interpretation.
- Clinical validation, diagnosis, treatment guidance, crisis-program design, or clinical sign-off.
- Instrument permission, a licence grant, or reproduction of protected items.
- Penetration testing, source-code remediation, formal accessibility certification, or production deployment.
- Access to patient records, real answers, scores, diagnoses, crisis interactions, or production health data.
- More than the fixed route or instrument-family limit without a separately reviewed scope.

## If sensitive information arrives by mistake

1. Stop reviewing the message or attachment. Do not copy its contents into notes, tickets, analytics, AI tools, or the review report.
2. Do not claim deletion, containment, confidentiality, or regulatory handling unless that action has been verified.
3. Notify the owner using only the minimum business context needed; do not repeat the sensitive content.
4. Use the email, storage, or service provider's documented controls and obtain qualified privacy or legal guidance when the situation may create an obligation.
5. Ask the sender to restate the business request without patient records, answers, scores, diagnoses, credentials, or other sensitive information.
6. Do not resume until a safe, non-sensitive scope is confirmed.

Suggested acknowledgment:

> Please do not send patient records, assessment answers, scores, diagnoses, login credentials, or other sensitive information. I have stopped reviewing the material. I cannot make a deletion or regulatory-handling claim until the relevant provider controls and obligations are checked. Please resend only the public URLs, fictional staging boundary, instrument name, business question, and timeline.

## Stage 4: Five-day delivery workflow

### Day 0: Freeze the scope

- Save the accepted scope and the exact canonical URLs.
- Record the review date and public source URLs.
- Confirm that all staging content is fictional and non-sensitive.
- Create a working register containing no answers, scores, results, or protected instrument items.

### Day 1: Public journey and rights evidence

- Inventory canonical routes and entry states on desktop and mobile.
- Record informational versus interactive boundaries, redirects, crawl directives, and index status.
- Verify exact instrument versions against owner, publisher, official manual, government, or original research sources.
- Mark commercial, electronic, reproduction, translation, scoring, attribution, and permission terms as verified, unresolved, or blocked.

### Day 2: Privacy, network, and safety boundaries

- Compare public privacy claims with observable browser storage and network behavior using fictional or empty states only.
- Check third-party requests, referrer behavior, analytics isolation, sharing behavior, and local-storage disclosures.
- Check crisis actions, non-diagnostic language, result-copy boundaries, reviewer scope, and escalation points without generating a real result.

### Day 3: Accessibility, usability, and discovery

- Review keyboard entry, accessible names, focus behavior, touch targets, narrow-screen layout, printable states, headings, and landmarks.
- Check titles, descriptions, canonicals, sitemap inclusion, robots directives, structured data, internal discovery, and status codes.
- Label formal conformance testing and specialist review as outside scope.

### Day 4: Build the report

- Write the executive verdict.
- Separate verified evidence, inference, and unknowns.
- Prioritize release blockers before improvements.
- Link every material rights, clinical, privacy, crisis, security, accessibility, and regulatory statement to an authoritative source when available.
- Include exact reproduction steps that use no real health data.

### Day 5: Quality gate and delivery

- Run the report QA checklist below.
- Deliver through the owner-approved method.
- Send no customer material to advertising, analytics, affiliate, or unrelated AI systems.
- Record delivery time and business-level metrics only.

## Report structure

1. Scope and evidence boundary.
2. Executive verdict: ready, conditional, or blocked for the reviewed public journey only.
3. Verified evidence.
4. Supported inferences.
5. Unknowns.
6. Rights and source register.
7. Privacy and network findings.
8. Safety, crisis, and non-diagnostic findings.
9. Accessibility and usability observations.
10. Crawl, indexing, and public-discovery findings.
11. Prioritized release gates.
12. Specialist or owner decisions required.
13. Limitations and exclusions.

## Pre-delivery QA

- [ ] The report contains no patient records, answers, scores, diagnoses, crisis status, or protected questionnaire items.
- [ ] The reviewed routes and evidence date match the accepted scope.
- [ ] Every material factual claim is sourced or labeled unknown.
- [ ] Rights evidence names the exact version and authoritative owner or publisher source.
- [ ] No finding claims legal compliance, clinical validation, certification, licence approval, or guaranteed safety.
- [ ] Crisis information is current, direct, accessible, and appropriate to the reviewed location.
- [ ] Accessibility findings are observations, not formal conformance certification.
- [ ] Security findings are bounded observations, not penetration-test results.
- [ ] The priority order puts safety, privacy, rights, and misleading claims before growth or polish.
- [ ] The final report and delivery path were checked for accidental sensitive content.

## Privacy-safe business metrics

Track only what is needed to decide whether the offer works:

- Inquiry date and organization type.
- Business role of the contact.
- Qualified or not qualified, with a non-sensitive reason.
- Quoted price and paid amount.
- Decision date and broad objection category.
- Delivery hours and elapsed business days.
- Number of in-scope routes and one instrument-family name.
- Revision time and whether the buyer requested repeat work.

Do not record assessment behavior, answers, scores, diagnoses, patient identity, crisis status, or user-level analytics.

## Continue, change, or stop

- **Continue:** a qualified buyer pays for the bounded scope and it can be delivered safely.
- **Standardize:** three paid reviews repeat the same non-sensitive deliverable or blocker.
- **Narrow or reprice:** actual delivery repeatedly exceeds ten hours or the follow-up burden exceeds the stated scope.
- **Escalate:** the buyer needs qualified legal, clinical, security, accessibility, privacy, or rights-holder decisions.
- **Stop:** the buyer requires health data, unlicensed instrument use, certification, diagnosis, unsafe crisis automation, or a misleading claim.
