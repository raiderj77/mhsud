# Professional Screening-Implementation Review: Decision Brief

**Status:** Local concept and validation brief. Not published, offered, priced, or available for purchase.

**Objective:** Create the lowest-risk path from MindCheckTools' strict-YMYL operating work to revenue without advertising beside health content, collecting assessment data, or prematurely building a hosted clinical platform.

## Recommendation

Validate a fixed-scope **technical screening-implementation readiness review** before building a paid printable, hosted API, or licensed platform. The service would review public or fictional staging journeys and return a prioritized evidence register. It would not receive patient records, assessment answers, scores, or production health data.

This is the best first monetization hypothesis because it uses work MindCheckTools already has to maintain, can produce direct buyer conversations, and can reveal which professional workflow is worth productizing. It also avoids competing directly with free government implementation toolkits and avoids the security, legal, support, and instrument-rights burden of a hosted measurement-based-care product.

## Current evidence

### Verified

- SAMHSA publishes free implementation guidance for both SBIRT programs and behavioral-health screening in schools. A generic paid implementation checklist would therefore compete with authoritative no-cost material.
- Commercial behavioral-health platforms already sell assessment, EHR, reporting, and implementation workflows to providers. Public examples use subscription, usage-based, or custom-quote models, which supports a professional software market but does not establish demand for MindCheckTools specifically.
- MindCheckTools now has a production-tested workflow for public-route inventory, instrument-rights classification, citation review, crisis and non-diagnostic QA, privacy-safe browser processing, sensitive-route isolation, accessibility checks, and crawl/index verification.
- The current site does not need to receive answers or scores to review those implementation controls.

### Inference

- A bounded review can be sold and delivered with less capital and regulatory exposure than a hosted assessment API.
- Repeated review findings can identify a narrower product worth building later, such as a self-hosted QA package or customer-controlled assessment shell.
- A generic printable is unlikely to be the strongest first offer unless buyer interviews identify a specific missing workflow that free authoritative toolkits do not solve.

### Unknown

- Qualified demand, buyer title, budget, sales cycle, price sensitivity, insurance needs, and willingness to buy from MindCheckTools.
- Whether buyers most value rights research, privacy/network QA, accessibility, crisis safeguards, implementation workflow, or a combined review.
- Which findings require paid legal, privacy, security, or topic-qualified clinical review.

## Proposed fixed scope

### Inputs

- Public URLs or a fictional/non-sensitive staging environment.
- The customer's declared instrument list and source/version records.
- Existing public privacy, consent, accessibility, crisis, and reviewer disclosures.

### Deliverable

- Public-route and entry-state inventory.
- Evidence and instrument-rights gap register using authoritative sources without reproducing restricted items.
- Privacy and third-party-network boundary review.
- Crisis, non-diagnostic, reviewer-scope, accessibility, SEO/indexing, and release-gate findings.
- Prioritized remediation backlog that labels verified evidence, inference, and unknowns.

### Hard exclusions

- No patient records, real assessment answers, scores, diagnoses, treatment decisions, or individualized recommendations.
- No legal opinion, HIPAA certification, clinical validation, penetration test, accessibility certification, or instrument permission grant.
- No claim that a review makes a product clinically safe, legally compliant, licensed, or suitable for diagnosis.
- No production change, outreach, account access, or external publication without exact authorization.

## Validation sequence

1. Approve one buyer segment and one fixed-scope deliverable.
2. Obtain scope review from an appropriate lawyer and topic-qualified clinical reviewer before public claims or delivery.
3. Draft a private sample report using MindCheckTools itself and fictional/non-sensitive evidence.
4. Validate demand through owner-approved one-to-one conversations or an approved marketplace listing; do not mass-message or automate outreach.
5. Set price only after at least three qualified conversations or one paid pilot supplies real willingness-to-pay evidence.
6. Productize only the repeated, non-sensitive parts of paid work. Prefer self-hosted or customer-controlled delivery before any hosted patient-data workflow.

## Success and stop rules

- **Proceed:** at least one paid pilot or three qualified buyers independently confirm the same urgent problem, buyer, and acceptable scope.
- **Revise:** interest exists but the requested outcome depends on legal or clinical certification outside current scope.
- **Stop:** buyers only want a free checklist, patient-data hosting, unlicensed instrument administration, diagnosis, or unsafe crisis automation.

## Genuine owner decisions

1. Choose the first buyer segment and approve the bounded service scope.
2. Approve any legal, clinical, privacy, security, insurance, or accessibility review spend.
3. Approve the exact public offer, price, terms, refund policy, and publication.
4. Approve each outreach message, marketplace listing, contract, payment account, or customer engagement before external action.
