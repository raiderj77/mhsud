# MindCheckTools Sellability and Professional Review Decision Brief

**Verified:** 2026-08-29
**Status:** The $495 Privacy and Screening Readiness Review is publicly listed. This brief records what is sellable now, what should be tested, and what should not be built. Publication is not proof of demand or product-market fit.

## Executive verdict

MindCheckTools should sell one bounded service before building another product: the existing **$495 Privacy and Screening Readiness Review** for digital-health and behavioral-health software teams. It uses public pages or fictional staging states, accepts no health data, and turns the site's existing rights, privacy, safety, accessibility, and release-gate work into a deliverable.

The next product should not be chosen until paid reviews reveal a repeated buyer problem. A small original screening-implementation evidence pack is the strongest product hypothesis, but it is a **cheap test**, not a build commitment. Generic HIPAA consulting, a hosted screening platform, published-instrument bundles, and a broad therapy-worksheet subscription should not be built now.

## Verified current state

- The public professional page lists a founding-client fixed scope of **$495**: up to five public or fictional staging routes, one named screening journey or instrument family, a written report, and one follow-up email.
- The service explicitly excludes patient records, real answers, scores, diagnoses, legal advice, clinical validation, instrument licensing, penetration testing, formal WCAG conformance, code remediation, and deployment.
- A fictional sample report, free screening-implementation checklist, and public instrument-rights guide support the offer.
- The inquiry path is a prefilled email to `hello@mindchecktools.com`; the website does not collect payment.
- MindCheckTools has original educational tools and worksheets, but original authorship alone does not establish clinical quality, buyer demand, or readiness for organization licensing.
- Published screening instruments remain subject to exact-version owner or publisher terms. No published instrument should be sold, sublicensed, modified, or bundled on the strength of public availability alone.
- Display advertising is permanently excluded. Sensitive answers, scores, diagnoses, crisis status, and tool behavior must never drive marketing or sales.
- Bookshop affiliate links are limited to the general recommended-reading page. Current affiliate earnings are unknown.

## Buyer and problem definition

### First buyer hypothesis

The narrowest credible buyer is a founder, product lead, engineering lead, privacy lead, or clinical-operations lead at a small digital-health or behavioral-health software company preparing a public screening journey for release.

### Problem

Evidence and release controls are scattered across instrument-owner terms, public claims, privacy/network behavior, crisis and non-diagnostic copy, accessibility, and indexing. A small team may need a single gap register before it pays lawyers, clinicians, security testers, accessibility specialists, or rights holders for decisions that only those specialists can make.

### Why MindCheckTools could be chosen

- Screening-specific public-journey focus rather than a generic HIPAA or security audit.
- Rights-aware evidence organization without reproducing restricted questionnaire items.
- A strict no-health-data review boundary.
- One small fixed scope and sample deliverable.

These are credible differentiators, not proven advantages. There are no verified paid customers, conversions, or testimonials yet.

## Cheapest reliable validation

1. Keep the fixed $495 offer and no-health-data intake boundary.
2. With owner approval for each external action, hold ten qualified one-to-one conversations with people responsible for digital-health screening releases. Do not mass-message.
3. Offer the same bounded scope and price; do not discount in exchange for a testimonial or claim that interest is a sale.
4. Record only business-level data: role, organization type, problem, objection, decision, delivery hours, and amount paid. Do not record patient or assessment data.
5. Continue if at least one buyer pays or three independent qualified buyers identify the same urgent problem and acceptable scope. A paid engagement is stronger evidence.
6. After three paid reviews, productize only a repeated non-sensitive deliverable. Stop or redesign if prospects want certification, legal advice, clinical validation, patient-data access, or unlicensed instrument use.

## Sales-readiness gates

Before accepting payment or beginning a customer engagement, confirm:

- Written scope, deliverables, exclusions, timing, payment terms, cancellation/refund terms, and customer acceptance.
- A payment or invoicing method that does not ask customers to email card or bank information.
- Business and tax handling appropriate to the owner's jurisdiction.
- Whether professional liability, cyber, or other insurance is required or prudent.
- A no-health-data intake rule and a process for deleting or refusing sensitive material sent by mistake.
- Escalation boundaries for legal, clinical, security, privacy, accessibility, and instrument-rights questions.
- Delivery capacity and a secure method for returning the report.

These are operational gates, not claims that any particular contract, insurance, tax, or compliance requirement applies. Qualified professional advice may be needed.

## 30/60/90-day decision path

### Days 1–30

- Verify the mailbox and owner-approved invoicing/payment path.
- Prepare a plain-language engagement scope and cancellation/refund terms for qualified review.
- Run owner-approved, individual demand conversations; track business-level outcomes only.
- Accept at most one paid founding-client review after the gates above are satisfied.

### Days 31–60

- Deliver the first review using public or fictional evidence only.
- Record actual delivery hours, objections, requested outcomes, and unresolved specialist needs.
- Do not add scope silently; price additional work separately only after written agreement.

### Days 61–90

- Continue only if there is paid demand or repeated qualified evidence.
- After three paid reviews, decide whether the repeated value is the service itself, an evidence pack, a narrow organization licence, or none of these.
- Build no API, hosted platform, or broad resource catalog without paid evidence.
